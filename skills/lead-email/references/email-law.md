# Email law, short version (not legal advice)

Read 2026-10-23. Check the links if anything here seems out of date.

## United States: CAN-SPAM

Source: FTC, "CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business"
https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business

- Covers all commercial email, including business to business. "The law makes no exception for
  business-to-business email."
- Do not use false or misleading header information (From, To, Reply-To).
- Do not use deceptive subject lines.
- Identify the message as an ad. (A personal one-to-one sales email that is plainly a pitch
  meets this in practice; ask a lawyer if you send in bulk.)
- Include a valid physical postal address.
- Tell recipients how to opt out, and honor it within 10 business days. Keep the opt-out
  working for at least 30 days after sending.
- You stay responsible even if someone else sends for you.
- Each separate email that breaks the law can cost up to $53,088 (FTC figure on that page).

## Canada: CASL

Source: CRTC, "Guidance on Implied Consent" https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm

- You need consent, express or implied, before sending a commercial email.
- Implied consent by "conspicuous publication": the person published their address (for example
  on their company site), there is no note saying they do not want commercial email, and your
  message is about their role or business. Keep a dated record of where you saw it.
- Existing business relationship: 2 years after a purchase or contract, 6 months after an
  inquiry.
- Every message needs who you are and an unsubscribe that works; stop within 10 business days.

## United Kingdom: PECR and UK GDPR

Source: ICO, "Business-to-business marketing"
https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/

- Emailing a company (a "corporate subscriber"): PECR consent rule does not apply, but you must
  say who you are and give a working opt-out. Keep a do-not-email list.
- Sole traders and some partnerships count as individuals: you need consent or the "soft
  opt-in" (an existing customer, similar products, opt-out offered).
- If the address names a person (jane.doe@company), UK GDPR applies: have a lawful basis, tell
  them where you got their details, and stop if they object.
- The ICO notes this guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act.

## European Union: GDPR plus each country's ePrivacy law

- A named work email is personal data. You need a lawful basis (often legitimate interest for
  B2B), must tell people where you got their data, and must stop when they object.
- Several EU countries require prior consent even for B2B email. Check the country before
  sending, and ask a local lawyer for anything at scale.
